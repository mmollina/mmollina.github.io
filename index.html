Genetic mapping · Polyploids

Multi-family linkage maps in polyploids

Creator and lead developer of MAPpoly and MAPpoly2.

Problem
Breeding programs often work with several related crosses. Linkage maps built one family at a time contain partly different sets of markers and use only the information within each family.
Approach
MAPpoly2 extends the single-family models in MAPpoly to interconnected diploid and autopolyploid families. A hidden Markov model builds a map for each parent. These are combined into a joint map, which is then recomputed to place the remaining markers. Ploidy levels 2, 4, and 6 can be combined in any configuration, and the computationally intensive steps are written in C++.
Contribution
Integrated linkage maps that combine information across interconnected families, implemented as open-source R software. The example output below is a consensus map for a multiparental potato population.

Recent methods development

Maternal maps from open-pollinated families

Problem
In open-pollinated families the mother is known and genotyped, but the fathers are not observed. Persistent paternal haplotype-sharing tracts, as arise when some pollen parents are related to the mother, can otherwise be absorbed into the maternal recombination map.
Approach
A source-aware hidden Markov model pairs the transmitted maternal homolog with a paternal source state, using the offspring genotypes and the genotyped mother. Paternal haplotype-sharing tracts are modelled explicitly. These states describe shared tracts statistically; they do not identify fathers. Intervals without linkage are reported as gaps rather than as large distances.
Contribution
HalfSibMap, an R package that builds maternal linkage maps and reconstructs each offspring's maternal inheritance in diploid open-pollinated families.
Role
Creator and lead developer, with co-authors Kim McKeever, Molly Irvin, and Fikret Isik.
Status
Available from GitHub; a methodological manuscript is in preparation.

Genomics · Sweetpotato

Inheritance and genome architecture of hexaploid sweetpotato

Problem
Hexaploid sweetpotato has six sets of chromosomes (2n = 6x = 90), making inheritance analysis more complex. Because of this, genetic studies in sweetpotato lagged well behind those in major diploid crops.
Approach
Ultra-dense multilocus mapping in a full-sib population from the cross Beauregard × Tanzania, using MAPpoly and more than 30,000 informative SNPs. Genotype probabilities were computed across the genome for every individual, and the complete hexaploid haplotypes of the offspring were inferred.
Contribution
An ultra-dense integrated genetic map with 96.5% collinearity with the diploid relative Ipomoea trifida. Inheritance was hexasomic in all linkage groups, apart from low levels of preferential pairing in linkage group 2; 73.3% of meiotic configurations were resolved as bivalents and 15.7% showed multivalent signatures. Related work includes a chapter on genetic maps in sweetpotato and, as a co-author, a phased chromosome-level genome assembly.

Visualization

Exploring polyploid genetic analyses in one place

Conceptual lead of VIEWpoly.

Problem
Results from linkage, QTL, and genome analyses in polyploids often sit in separate tools.
Approach
VIEWpoly is an interactive application that brings them together, so users can explore candidate genes in a genome browser. It reads output from MAPpoly, polymapR, diaQTL, QTLpoly, and polyqtlR.
Contribution
One place to explore linkage, QTL, and genomic results from five polyploid analysis packages, published in the Journal of Open Source Software (2022) and distributed on CRAN.

Interactive explorations in quantitative genetics

Quantitative genetics brings together statistical inference, genetic theory, and computation to understand biological variation and inform breeding decisions. These explorations take three ideas from that much larger field and make them tangible with simulated data.

Mixed models · Multi-environment trials

How do environmental and field effects influence estimates of genetic merit?

What is measured on a plot reflects more than genetic merit. The environment of the trial, the plot’s position in the field, and the way each genotype responds to that environment all contribute. Growing the same genotypes in several environments makes it possible to tell these sources apart. Here a mixed model splits three simulated trials into their parts.

Relationship matrices · Pedigree and markers

What can genomic information reveal about expected genetic relationships?

A pedigree describes the expected genetic relationship between individuals, while genomic markers provide an estimate of their genetic relationship based on observed marker information. The two are related but not identical: relatives do not share exactly the expected fraction of their genomes, and markers measure that fraction with some error. Comparing them shows this variation and, occasionally, pairs that do not fit the pedigree at all.

Reaction norms · Selection across environments

Is the best genotype the same in every environment?

Genotypes can differ in how they respond to the environment. When they do, the differences between them change from one environment to the next, sometimes only in size and sometimes in order. About

I am a quantitative geneticist trained in plant genetics and breeding. My doctoral work at the University of São Paulo developed models for building genetic maps in autopolyploids, with applications in sugarcane. Since then I have focused on algorithms for genomic analysis in complex family structures, especially outcrossing diploid and polyploid crops.

At North Carolina State University I led research on statistical methods and computational algorithms for diploid and polyploid populations, created MAPpoly and MAPpoly2, and worked with research groups and breeding programs in sweetpotato, potato, blackberry, forage crops, and other species, combining statistical models with R and C++ software.

Since October 2025 I have worked at AbacusBio on quantitative genetics and biometrics for commercial breeding applications across horticultural crops, forest trees, and livestock.
